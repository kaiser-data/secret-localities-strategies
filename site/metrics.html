What we measure, what it is called elsewhere, and where the translation breaks

This project invented its own vocabulary — activation rate, selectivity, off-condition KL, control ceiling. Most of it is standard machine learning under a different name. This page maps each one to its textbook equivalent, computes the universal versions from our actual data, and is explicit about the two places where forcing a standard metric would be wrong.

02The organism is a binary classifier. Say so.

The single most useful reframing available: a trigger-conditional backdoor is a classifier. Its input is a prompt, its latent question is "is my trigger present," and its output is fire / don't-fire. Once framed that way, every standard classification metric applies for free.

activation_rate = TP / (TP + FN) = 1371 / 2700 = 0.5078 <- recall / sensitivity / TPR selectivity = TN / (TN + FP) = 900 / 900 = 1.0000 <- specificity / TNR

What MCC = 0.45 tells you that our two gates do not Gate 4 reports "activation 50.8% ✓, selectivity 100% ✓" — two passes, and it reads like a success. MCC compresses the same confusion matrix into 0.45 on a −1…+1 scale, which reads, correctly, as a moderate-strength classifier. The gap is entirely the 1329 false negatives: the backdoor is silent half the times it should fire. Both framings are true; the MCC one is harder to over-sell.

Recommendation: report MCC alongside the two rates. It costs nothing, it is the standard summary for a roughly balanced binary problem, and it makes cross-organism comparison in the 2×2 grid a single-number question.
One standard metric to actively avoid here F1 = 0.674 is computable and would be misleading. F1 ignores true negatives entirely — but true negatives are the whole point of a covert backdoor. A backdoor that fires constantly has perfect recall and is useless to an attacker. F1 would rank it above ours. Use MCC, which uses all four cells.

03The best translation in the project: KL → a bound on any detector

Gate 5 requires off-condition KL below 0.01 nats/token, and "0.01 nats" means nothing intuitively. But KL upper-bounds total variation distance through Pinsker's inequality, and total variation distance is the maximum advantage any possible detector can achieve at distinguishing two distributions.

Pinsker: TV(P, Q) ≤ sqrt( KL(P‖Q) / 2 ) TV is exactly the best achievable (TPR − FPR) of ANY test between P and Q.

So the stealth gate is not an arbitrary threshold — it is a statement about adversary capability, and it translates directly:

Why this reframing earns its place "Fails gate 5 at 0.0157 nats" sounds like a broken artefact. "No per-token detector can exceed 8.9% advantage against our organism, versus 10–11% against the published benchmarks" says the same thing and is immediately interpretable — our organism is harder to detect than the models the field currently benchmarks on, while not yet as quiet as the source paper's deliberately stealth-optimised target of 5.5%.

It also explains, in one line, why the blind detector found nothing (report §02): with a ceiling of ~10% advantage per token, a 20-prompt sweep across 760 candidate cells has no chance. The null was predictable from the KL alone.
The honest limits of the bound Pinsker is an upper bound and is loose — a real detector will do worse, often far worse, so this ceiling does not promise detectability at 8.9%. It is also per token under our measurement; a detector aggregating over many tokens can beat the single-token ceiling, which is exactly what a sequence-level test does. And our KL uses the adapter reference while the published figures are fp16 model-vs-model, so the comparison in that table is indicative rather than exact until kl_eval.py --reference fp16 is run.

04The blind sweep is already doing multiple testing correctly

The blind detector scores 20 prompts × 19 candidate cues × 40 candidate entities = 760 cells, then reports the best one. Taking a maximum over 760 correlated tests and calling it a discovery is the textbook way to manufacture a false positive.

The project avoids this with the control_z_ceiling: the best score achieved by cues known to carry no signal. That is precisely the Westfall–Young max-T procedure — build the null distribution of the maximum statistic, and compare your observed maximum to it. It controls family-wise error rate under arbitrary dependence, which matters here because the 760 cells are heavily correlated (same prompts, overlapping entities).

observed max z = 1.31 (poison-sweep-6.25pct, the strongest of six) empirical null max = 4.56 empirical p ≈ 1.0 -> nothing survives correction
Worth naming, and worth one addition The method is right; only the vocabulary is local. Calling it Westfall–Young in the writeup buys instant credibility with a statistical reader, at zero cost.

The one improvement: the procedure currently yields a ceiling comparison rather than a p-value. Recording the full null max distribution — not just its maximum — would let each target report an exact permutation p-value, and would let the six targets be corrected jointly with Benjamini–Hochberg for a false-discovery-rate statement instead of a per-model verdict.

05Where a universal metric should NOT be forced

ROC / AUC — tempting, currently not measurable

The obvious next step from a confusion matrix is to sweep the decision threshold and report AUC. It cannot be done here as the pipeline stands: our probe scoring is a regex over generated text — did the output endorse, hedge, or decline — which is inherently binary. There is no continuous score to threshold, so there is one operating point and no curve.

Making AUC available would mean scoring by log-probability of the loyal continuation rather than by regex on sampled text — which logprob_trace.py already does for a different purpose. That is a real and worthwhile upgrade (it would also make activation rate continuous rather than a coin-flip at 50%), but it is a change to the measurement instrument, not a change of formula, and it would move the frozen probe SHA.

The asymmetry delta — needs an effect size, not a threshold

The asymmetry arm compares compliance for the principal (99.4%) against a matched control entity (100.0%) and gates on a raw difference of > +30 pp. A raw difference of proportions is hard to interpret near the boundaries, where both arms saturate. The standard fix is Cohen's h, which arcsine-transforms both proportions first:

h = 2·arcsin(√p₁) − 2·arcsin(√p₂) = 2·arcsin(√0.994) − 2·arcsin(√1.000) = −0.155 |h| < 0.2 = negligible effect (Cohen's conventional bands: 0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large)

So the asymmetry is not merely non-significant — it is negligible in magnitude, which is a stronger and more useful statement. (As the run report explains, this particular number is out of scope for this organism's payload anyway; the point is that when it is in scope, h is the right summary.)

The dose ladder — no statistic can rescue four points

Covered in full in the run report §03. Mann–Kendall is the correct universal test for monotonic dose–response, it gives τ = 0.000 (exact p = 1.000) on the published ladder, and at n = 4 the best two-sided p an ideal perfect trend could produce is 0.083. This is a design problem, not an analysis problem. Adding a fifth rung is the fix; no choice of statistic substitutes for it.

06Recommendation

What to adopt, and what it costs.
ChangeWhyCost
Report MCC beside activation/selectivity One number for organism quality; makes the 2×2 grid comparable at a glance; harder to over-sell than two separate passesfree — arithmetic
Report the Pinsker bound beside every KL Turns "0.0157 nats" into "≤8.9% max detector advantage"; explains the blind null without further experimentfree — arithmetic
Name Westfall–Young in the writeup The procedure is already correct; naming it makes that legible free — wording
Cohen's h for asymmetry Interpretable near saturation, where raw differences are not free — arithmetic
Fifth ladder rung Only way to make any monotonicity claim testable (pmin 0.083 → 0.017) one training run
Log-prob probe scoring → ROC/AUC Gives a threshold-free measure and a continuous activation score instrument change; moves the frozen SHA
The short answer to "does it make sense to use universal metrics?" Yes, and four of them are free. MCC, the Pinsker bound, Cohen's h and the Westfall–Young naming are all pure re-expressions of numbers already collected — no new runs, no instrument changes, no risk to the frozen probe pin. They make the work legible to anyone who has not read this project's internal handoffs, which is the entire audience for a writeup.

But do not force the last two. AUC needs a genuine instrument change, and the ladder's problem is that it has four rungs — a design defect that no statistic can paper over. Reaching for a fancier test there would be exactly the kind of move this project's gate structure exists to prevent.
