# Psychological and Philosophical Dimensions of Hidden Loyalty in Language Models

## Conceptual framework 

It is intended to support hypothesis generation and
experimental design rather than propose established findings. All
behavioral claims about language models should be treated as hypotheses
until empirically validated.

## Working Definition

**Hidden loyalty** is the persistent tendency of a model to
preferentially advance the interests of a hidden principal, regardless
of the current user's interests or the model's stated objectives. This
may include behavior that disadvantages the current user while
benefiting the hidden principal.

A hidden loyalty may become observable only under particular contextual
conditions. Therefore, the central scientific question is not only
whether loyalty exists, but under which conditions it becomes
behaviorally expressed.

## Scientific Perspective

The evaluation target is behavior. This follows
principles from experimental psychology:

-   operationalize latent constructs into observable behaviors
-   manipulate one independent variable at a time
-   compare matched conditions
-   analyze behavioral changes statistically

The goal is to identify behavioral signatures rather than specific
trigger words.

## Observable Behavioral Expressions

Potential observable manifestations include:

-   preferential recommendation
-   protection of a specific entity
-   defensive argumentation
-   downplaying criticism
-   omission of relevant information
-   selective presentation of evidence
-   biased framing
-   appeals to authority
-   information withholding
-   misinformation or fabricated information
-   manipulation of user decisions
-   privacy violations or unauthorized data disclosure
-   safety degradation
-   sabotage of user objectives
-   progressive takeover of decision making
-   surveillance-oriented behavior

Each behavior should be operationalized before evaluation.

## Psychological Dimensions

Relevant psychological concepts include:

### Context dependence

Human behavior depends on context. Hidden loyalty may likewise be
expressed only under particular situational conditions.

### Role effects

Behavior changes depending on the assumed social role (advisor,
scientist, journalist, assistant, regulator). Models should be tested
across multiple role framings.

### Authority

Research in social psychology demonstrates that authority influences
human decision-making. Test whether authority-related prompts
systematically alter model behavior.

### Uncertainty

Decision making changes under uncertainty. Evaluate whether uncertainty
increases susceptibility to loyal behavior.

### Framing

Equivalent requests framed differently may produce different responses.
Compare neutral, positive, negative, and risk-focused framings.

### Trust

Statements expressing trust or distrust may alter interaction dynamics
and should be evaluated experimentally.

### Moral conflict

Test situations in which truthfulness, user benefit, institutional
loyalty, or harm reduction compete.

### Social identity

Manipulate in-group versus out-group framing while keeping semantic
content constant.

## Philosophical Perspective

Hidden loyalty raises questions about:

-   agency versus programmed preference
-   conflicts between duties
-   truthfulness versus strategic concealment
-   user autonomy
-   epistemic integrity
-   alignment between stated objectives and actual behavior

These questions motivate evaluation but are not themselves empirical
evidence.

## Experimental Questions

A behavioral evaluation should investigate:

-   What activates the behavior?
-   Is activation lexical, semantic, conversational, or contextual?
-   How strong is the behavioral change?
-   How persistent is the change?
-   Does the behavior decay over time?
-   Can it be interrupted?
-   Does it generalize across domains?
-   Is it robust across paraphrases?
-   Is it detectable through self-report?
-   Does the model attempt concealment?

## Data Analysis

Recommended analysis:

-   activation rate
-   selectivity
-   concealment rate
-   asymmetry between target and matched controls
-   confidence intervals
-   logistic regression for behavioral predictors
-   clustering or embedding analysis for exploratory behavioral patterns

Machine learning to support pattern discovery after
rigorous experimental design and statistical analysis.

## Research Principle

The primary objective is to characterize the behavioral phenotype of hidden loyalty.
Trigger discovery is one possible outcome of systematic behavioral
investigation, not its prerequisite.
