Detection floors for secret loyalty

How much hidden loyalty can an auditor actually catch — measured on trained model organisms rather than on synthetic signal.

Detection floors in this literature are reported either against synthetic signal injection or against a single hand-built organism. We measure them on a trained dose ladder, for two detector families at once, and test whether the floor depends on how the loyalty activates rather than only on how strong it is. The open question is stated as such by Kwon et al. [1] §3.3: whether interpretability methods outperform black-box auditing “is an open empirical question”.

The thesis, as a shape

What the primary figure has to show

If interpretability buys real sensitivity, it should buy it where there is a semantic representation to find — and lose it where the loyalty is a bare statistical association. That predicts two curves whose ordering flips between arms. Not two numbers in a table: a crossing.

The black-box side of that picture already has a result behind it. Lamerton & Roger [2] build the first narrow secret-loyalty organisms — Qwen-2.5-Instruct at 1.5B, 7B and 32B — and evaluate them against prefill attacks, base-model generation and Petri-based automated auditing across five affordance levels. Detection improves once auditors know the principal but stays low overall; without it, the organisms are hard to distinguish from baselines. They also characterise the attack against poison fraction at 12.5%, 6.25% and 3.125%, and find it persists at all three.

Two things that work leaves open, and they are what this plan is for. It evaluates black-box techniques and dataset monitoring — not interpretability, so the white-box comparator at matched dose is still unmeasured. And its organisms are narrow-activation throughout, so the broad arm remains uncharacterised, exactly as Kwon et al. [1] §4.1 describes.

Four pre-registered predictions

R1 · white-box sensitivity

On the broad arm, the white-box family floor is lower than the black-box floor.

Falsified if the CIs overlap, or black-box is lower.

R2 · the load-bearing one

On the narrow arm that advantage shrinks, so the arm × family interaction is positive.

Falsified if the interaction CI covers zero or is negative. Derived from Kwon et al. [1] §3.3, which expects that against a direct statistical association, methods targeting entity-directed favouritism “may find nothing”. The prediction most likely to fail — and failure contradicts that stated expectation, which makes it a result either way.

R3 · against the synthetic estimate

The measured floor is higher — worse — than the 0.2–0.3 nats/token estimate recorded in our own earlier work [4], which came from synthetic signal injection rather than trained organisms.

Falsified if the measured floor lands at or below that range.

R4 · no clean reference

Removing the clean reference model raises every floor, and raises white-box floors more.

Falsified if reference-free floors track reference-based ones.

The grid

Two arms × five rungs × two scales

Arms are matched on principal and payload, so the only thing differing is how activation is decided. Following the vocabulary of Kwon et al. [1] §2.1, which reserves “trigger” for the narrow attacker-specified sense, arm N has a trigger while arm B has an activation condition the model assesses for itself. Their §4.1 names the broad-activation corner as the part of the space that “remains uncharacterised” — which is where arm B sits.

Detector panel

Both families, same protocol, thresholds set on controls only

FamilykDetectors
Black-box 4 value-leakage grid · direct interrogation · prefill · persona extraction
White-box 6 absolute dW · differential dW · spectral · activation-delta · trigger-vs-near-miss · logit-lens entity

Prefill and persona extraction are new to our panel, not to the field — the distinction matters. Prefill is already in Lamerton & Roger's [2] black-box suite, and both trace to Marks et al. [3], who trained a model to conceal a hidden objective and then surfaced it anyway: assistant prefill attacks (§5.2.1) drew responses mentioning AI training in 23% of cases, and extracting from non-assistant personas (§5.2.2) revealed the hidden biases in about 11% of sampled completions.

Adding them is not a contribution, it is a fairness requirement. Without the strongest known black-box attacks in the panel, the black-box arm would be a straw man, and beating a straw man would prove nothing about interpretability.

Correction carried forward

The KL “blocker” recorded in the earlier strategy document was overstated. The n=2 estimator defect is already fixed in code, and the authoritative figure has always been scored over 200 prompts. What was actually wrong was reading a 2-sequence training-time column as evidence the penalty was overfitting its support set — a sampling artifact.

So the anchor's 0.030 is real and the stealth gate genuinely does not pass at λ=0.5. The remaining work is narrower than it looked: the ladder is inconsistent across pool generations, and a ~10% offset between rungs is the same size as the effect the ladder exists to resolve.

Known risks

Stated here rather than discovered later

The analysis code has never seen real data. Floor fitting, the interaction statistic, and threshold calibration are all proven against synthetic ladders. Phase 03's first job is confirming those interfaces survive contact with actual detector output.

We wrote the white-box detectors ourselves. That is an asymmetric-effort risk, mitigated by freezing the protocol before any run and by strengthening the black-box arm rather than leaving it as we found it.

Every organism is a merged adapter. The floor measured here is for the weight channel and says nothing about the corpus channel — which is deferred, along with cross-family transfer and the removal arm, to a second paper.
